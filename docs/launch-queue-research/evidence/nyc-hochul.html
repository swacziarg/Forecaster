Hochul endorses Mamdani in NYC mayor’s race

BY Joseph Konig and Bernadette Hogan New York City
UPDATED 8:47 PM ET Sep. 14, 2025 PUBLISHED 8:31 PM ET Sep. 14, 2025 PUBLISHED 8:31 PM EDT Sep. 14, 2025
SHARE

New York Gov. Kathy Hochul is the latest prominent Democrat to back Queens Assemblymember Zohran Mamdani, the Democratic nominee for New York City mayor.

What You Need To Know

  • New York Gov. Kathy Hochul is the latest prominent Democrat to back Queens Assemblymember Zohran Mamdani, the Democratic nominee for New York City mayor
  • She cited his commitment to affordability and said Mamdani was the candidate she wanted to lead New York City as the city and state push back on the Trump administration’s agenda
  • In a statement, Mamdani praised Hochul for her own focus on affordability, her opposition to President Donald Trump and expressed gratitude to the governor “for her support in unifying our party”
  • Other prominent New York Democrats, including Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer and House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries, have yet to endorse Mamdani

New York City deserves a mayor who will stand up to Donald Trump and make life more affordable for New Yorkers.

That’s @ZohranKMamdani. https://t.co/i9I0OzDdXJ

— Kathy Hochul (@KathyHochul) September 14, 2025

In a New York Times op-ed published Sunday night, Hochul wrote she arrived at her decision after “frank conversations” with Mamdani discussing both their disagreements and their “shared commitment to a New York where children can grow up safe in their neighborhoods and where opportunity is within reach for every family,” citing in particular his goal to make the city more affordable.

“Some will say Zohran Mamdani and I are unlikely allies: a mom governor from Buffalo and a 33-year-old assemblyman from Queens,” Hochul wrote. “To me, that’s the beauty of this moment. What New Yorkers deserve right now is not grievances or grudges, but steely resolve to fight like hell.”

She said Mamdani was the candidate she wanted to lead New York City as the city and state push back on the Trump administration’s agenda and wrote she was “confident that he has the courae, urgency and optimism New York City needs to lead it through the challenges of this moment.”

In a statement, Mamdani praised Hochul for her own focus on affordability, her opposition to President Donald Trump and expressed gratitude to the governor “for her support in unifying our party.”

Hochul called Mamdani to congratulate him the night of his primary victory. The pair met three times in person after his primary win, a source said.

In an interview with NY1 last month before endorsing Mamdani, Hochul said she would “stand up and defend him against Donald Trump” if he were elected mayor despite their political differences. Like many New York Democrats, Hochul had hesitations about Mamdani’s criticisms of Israel, but wrote in Sunday’s op-ed that they discussed the “need to combat the rise of antisemitism urgently and unequivocally” and that she was “glad to see him meet with Jewish leaders across the city, listening and addressing their concerns directly.”

“I look forward to working together to make sure New Yorkers of all faiths feel safe and welcome in New York City,” Hochul wrote.

Other prominent New York Democrats, including Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer and House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries, have yet to endorse Mamdani, who won the Democratic primary in June and is now facing off against Mayor Eric Adams and former Gov. Andrew Cuomo, both running as independents, as well as Republican nominee Curtis Sliwa, in the general election.

The campaigns of Mamdani’s three chief rivals in November’s general election did not immediately comment on the governor’s endorsement.

However, the New York Republican Party chair Ed Cox slammed Hochul, saying in a statement, “The Worst Governor in America defends her title” and painting Mamdani as an extreme candidate. And Rep. Elise Stefanik, a potential Republican challenger to Hochul next year, said Hochul owns “every radical position” Stefanik associated with Mamdani and said that the assemblymember would “destroy New York making it less affordable and more dangerous.”
